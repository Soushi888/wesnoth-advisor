# 03 Banished

Legend of the Invincibles, Part I, NORMAL. Read off the live board on turn 1 (`LotI1-Banished-Auto-Save1`), the scenario file and the map.

## The shape of it

This is not a battle. It is a build-up with a battle stapled to the end.

You start in the far north-east corner at (37,2). Margund the Orcish Warlord sits in the far south-west at (20,43), **fifty hexes away**. He is the only enemy on the map, he has **zero gold**, his income is **zero**, and he is flagged as a guardian, which means he never moves off his hex.

Until you walk into one particular rectangle, that is the entire opposition. No patrols, no garrison, nothing. Thirty-three turns and an empty map.

The moment a unit of yours steps anywhere in **x 18 to 31, y 30 to 40**, the scenario changes: Margund is handed **250 gold and 15 income a turn**, four orcs appear at (18,35), (31,36), (15,37) and (26,41), and the objective flips from "explore the woods" to "kill Margund".

**That rectangle cannot be avoided.** I checked every route: with the box excluded there is no path from your corner to Margund at all. The furthest south you can get without touching it is y=36, hard against the eastern edge. So the second phase is compulsory. The only thing you control is *when*.

## The one number that drives every decision

Margund's army is **250 + 15 for every turn after you trigger**.

Trigger on turn 12 and he ends the scenario with about 570 gold of orcs. Trigger on turn 22 and he ends with about 415. Every turn you spend building before you step in the box is a turn he earns nothing, and every turn after is fifteen gold of grunts walking at you.

So: **go slow, then go fast.** Build for twenty turns, then hit hard.

Split into three or four small groups and sweep. Delenia has movement 9 and both Rogues have 6 to 7, so the fast units should take the long routes to the western and southern villages while the slow ones mop up the north-east cluster.

## The day comes back

Scenario 2 was locked at night. This one runs the full cycle, six turns long, starting at Afternoon:

| Turn | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | Afternoon **+25** | Dusk 0 | First Watch -25 | Second Watch -25 | Dawn 0 | Morning **+25** |

Daylight lands on turns **1, 6, 7, 12, 13, 18, 19, 24, 25, 30 and 31**.

**Every orc in this scenario is chaotic**, including Margund. So daylight is a fifty point swing in your favour, exactly like the night was last time, only now it points the other way. Plan the assault to land on a daylight turn and try to be defending, not attacking, through First and Second Watch.

This also means **the bench comes back**. Deoran and Evan (Bowmen), Meotharryn, Aeraent and Syry (Fencers), Hallaent (Spearman) and Aran (Heavy Infantryman) were dead weight in a permanent night. Here they are lawful units in a scenario against chaotic enemies with a daylight cycle. Recall them.

## The ground

The map is 46 by 46 and almost entirely forest. Your core units are elusivefoot: Delenia, both Rogues, every Thief and Footpad, and the Fencers. They are hit **30% of the time in forest, hills, villages and castle**, and 40% on open grass.

Two things follow.

**Never stand in the river.** Shallow water puts elusivefoot at **60% to be hit**, double the forest number. The Maelea River runs across the map and the labels point at it. Cross it, do not fight in it.

**Fight in the trees.** There is almost nowhere on this map where you are forced onto open ground, so there is no excuse for ever being at 40%.

**Three keeps.** Yours at (39,1), two hexes from where Efraim starts. Margund's at (20,43), ringed by six castle hexes so six orcs can defend him at once. And an **abandoned elvish keep at (23,33)** with castle around it, sitting inside the trigger box, 38 hexes from your start. Taking that keep gives you a forward recruiting base ten hexes from Margund, which is worth more than it sounds when your reinforcements would otherwise walk fifty hexes.

## Items

The bag holds five items, read off the board: two **Heathenbashers** (#74, mace), **Rherraent's Sword** (#113), **The Doctrine of Vindictiveness** (#336) and **Exterminator's Knives** (#804). Equip on turn 1: the item menu has no gate and there is nothing on the map that can punish you for spending a turn organising.

**Correct a number I got wrong last scenario.** An item's `damage` figure is a **percentage**, not a weapon rating. Twinkling Sword's "20" means +20% on Delenia's 7 damage base sword, giving 8, and Virtual Void's flat +4 takes it to 12. So none of these items is transformative; they are all single-digit bumps. Do the arithmetic before getting excited.

| Item | Goes to | What it is actually worth |

| **Heathenbasher** | **Aran**, Heavy Infantryman | +30% on his 11 damage mace, so **11x2 to 14x2**, plus arcane and impact resistance and the illuminates aura. Only one of the two can be worn; the second is dead weight until another mace user appears. |

### The Doctrine of Vindictiveness

It is not a weapon or armour. It occupies the "limited" slot and unlocks a three-step advancement chain, available only once a unit is past its maximum level and choosing an AMLA. Each step also gives +3 maximum hit points, a full heal, and raises that unit's experience bar by 20%.

| Step | Ability | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| DoV1 | triggerable | Attack damage +1 every time this unit **is hit**. The accumulated bonus halves each turn. |
| DoV2 | vindictive | Attack damage +1 every time an **adjacent ally is hit**. Same halving. |
| DoV3 | unforgiving | Attacks deal **50% extra fire damage** against any unit type that hit it in the last five turns. |

None of it counts damage taken in retaliation, so it only builds when the enemy attacks you on their turn.

**This is a front-line tank package, and you do not own a front-line tank.** It rewards standing there and absorbing hits. Delenia is a skirmisher who is hit 30% of the time in forest specifically so that she is not absorbing hits, and Efraim is the same shape. Putting it on Delenia costs nothing, because the limited slot has no competition, but do not plan around it: she is 85 experience from an advancement with a 125% modifier, so she almost certainly will not reach one this scenario. Hold it, and reconsider when someone on the roster actually becomes a wall.

**The Heathenbasher stops being a trap here.** Its illuminates aura pushes adjacent hexes twenty five points toward daylight. In scenario 2 that was suicide because everything you owned was chaotic. Here the enemy is chaotic too, so at night the aura takes twenty five points off every orc next to Aran while giving Aran himself twenty five. Build a small lawful squad around him, Bowmen and Fencers, and keep your Rogues out of his aura.

## What there is to loot

No fixed treasure on this map: no rare item drop, no placed chest, nothing like the dagger at (21,1) last time. What you get is **10% per orc killed, plus a 10% gem chance**.

That sounds thin, and per kill it is. But Margund will spend 250 gold immediately and 15 a turn after that, on units costing 12 to 28 gold. Over a long second phase he will field somewhere north of thirty units, and killing thirty orcs at 10% is about three items and three gems, which is the same haul scenario 2 gave you for far more dangerous work.

So the loot argument and the survival argument point the same way: let him spend his gold walking into your line, and kill everything. Do not rush past his army to snipe him unless the clock forces it.

## The plan

**Turns 1 to 3, organise.** Equip everything. Recall the whole roster including the lawful bench, which is usable again. Recruit to fill out the group, favouring **Poachers**: they are the only ranged unit you can buy and 33 hit points is respectable. Efraim can recruit at (39,1), two hexes away.

**Turns 3 to 18, sweep.** Three or four groups, no group without a real fighter in it. Take all 31 free villages. Your income should be north of thirty a turn by the halfway mark. Keep spending it: at 40% carryover, gold sitting in the bank at the end of the scenario is worth 40 pence in the pound.

**Do not clip the box.** The trigger fires on entry to x 18 to 31, y 30 to 40. Several of the long southern village runs pass near it. Route around, and remember that (24,38) is inside it, so that village is a phase-two objective, not a phase-one one.

**Turns 18 to 21, assemble.** Bring everything to the northern lip of the box, around y=29, spread across the map's width so the four spawned orcs cannot all fall on one group. Rest everyone to full. Check the calendar: you want to step in so that the fight starts on a **daylight** turn, which means entering on turn 18, 19, 24 or 25.

**The trigger.** One unit steps in, preferably a cheap recruit rather than Delenia, since this time there is no forced-miss protection and no reason to expose her. The shroud lifts over x 21 to 29, y 31 to 35 and you see the elvish fortress.

**Turns 19 to 26, take the elvish keep at (23,33) and hold.** It is inside the box, it has castle around it, and it is ten hexes from Margund. Recruiting there instead of at (39,1) is the difference between reinforcements arriving in one turn and arriving in six. Fight defensively in forest at 30% while the orcs come to you, and let Huwyn poison anything with a lot of hit points.

**Turns 26 to 31, kill Margund.** He is 78 hit points on a castle hex with six defended approaches. Delenia backstabbing in daylight does 30 a strike. He has **weak reflect**, which sends a quarter of every hit back at the attacker, so expect Delenia to take roughly 7 per landed strike. It cannot kill, so it is an annoyance rather than a threat, but it means your low-hit-point Thieves should not be the ones landing big blows on him.

**Leave two turns of slack.** Turn 33 arriving with Margund alive is a defeat, and there is no partial credit.

## What will actually hurt you

**Orcish Assassins.** 17 gold, and they throw poisoned knives. Poison on Efraim or Delenia is not lethal on its own but it strips them down while the rest of the army works. Keep both near a village once phase two starts, because villages cure poison.

**Trolls.** 25 gold, and they regenerate 8 a turn. Chip damage does nothing to them. Poison and focus fire are the answers.

**Goblin Knights and Wolf Riders.** Fast, and they will go around your line for your leader. Efraim is level 2 now with 44 hit points and real armour, but he is still a loss condition.

**The clock.** Thirty-three turns sounds like plenty until you notice Margund is fifty hexes away through forest that costs two movement a hex.

## Opening budget

166 gold, and 14 units on the recall list at 20 each. You cannot afford to bring everyone out, and you should not try.

Nothing on this map can hurt you for roughly eighteen turns, so the army you need on turn 1 is not a fighting army, it is **legs**. Buy movement now, recall the fighters later out of village income.

**Turn 1, about 124 gold:**

- Recall **Huwyn** (20) and **Ynoc** (20). Your two real strikers, and Huwyn carries the Poison.
- Recruit **six Footpads** at 14 each (84). Movement 7, cheaper than a recall, and every one of them is a veteran by scenario 4.
- That leaves about 42 in hand and ten units on the map counting Delenia and Efraim.

**Do not recall the lawful bench yet.** Deoran, Evan, Meotharryn, Aeraent, Syry, Hallaent, Aran and Mario are movement 5 to 7 and will contribute nothing for eighteen turns while costing upkeep the whole time. Recall them around turn 12 to 15, once village income is above twenty a turn, so they arrive fresh for the fight rather than tired from the walk.

**Watch upkeep.** Every non-loyal unit costs its level per turn. Delenia is loyal and Efraim is the leader, so both are free. Ten fielded units of mixed level is roughly ten a turn, which your first ten villages cover.

**Then spend everything.** Carryover is 40%, so a gold banked at the end is worth 40 pence. From turn 15 onward, convert income into Rogues at 25 and recalls at 20 until you are fielding as much as upkeep allows.
